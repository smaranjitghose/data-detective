## Two things moving together, and what that does not prove

Ice cream sales and drowning deaths rise and fall together across the year. The correlation is real and reproducible, and nobody thinks ice cream causes drowning. Summer causes both.

The example is worn out and the pattern behind it is not. Every dataset you work with contains relationships of exactly this shape, and telling them apart from causal ones is the hardest part of analysis.

## Four explanations for any correlation

When two variables move together, at least four things could be producing it.

| Explanation | Structure | Example |
| ------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Direct causation | X causes Y | More study hours cause higher marks |
| Reverse causation | Y causes X | Lower marks cause more study hours |
| Common cause | Z causes both X and Y | Summer causes ice cream sales and swimming |
| Chance | Neither, the data is coincidental | Two unrelated series both trending upward |

The correlation coefficient cannot distinguish between them. It is the same number in all four cases, which is why no amount of statistical sophistication applied to observational data settles the question on its own.

Reverse causation deserves more attention than it gets. It is often as plausible as the story people prefer, and it is rarely tested. Hospitals with more staff have worse patient outcomes, and the reason is that difficult cases get sent to well-staffed hospitals.

## A confounder in a file you know

The Palmer penguin data contains a striking case that needs no invented example.

Across all 342 birds, bill length and bill depth are negatively correlated at −0.235. Longer bills come with shallower ones.

Now compute the same correlation within each species:

| Species | $n$ | Correlation |
| --------- | --: | ----------: |
| Adelie | 151 | +0.391 |
| Chinstrap | 68 | +0.654 |
| Gentoo | 123 | +0.643 |

Every species shows a clear positive relationship. Pooled together, the sign flips.

The explanation is in the group averages. Gentoos have long bills, averaging 47.5 mm, and shallow ones, averaging 15.0 mm. Adelies have shorter bills at 38.8 mm and deeper ones at 18.3 mm. So the pooled cloud slopes downward as you move from one species to the other, even though each species cloud slopes upward internally.

> The negative correlation was real and it was about species, not about bills. Species was the third variable, and it was sitting in the file the whole time.

Anyone reporting the pooled figure would have described penguin bills backwards, using a correct calculation on complete data.

## Finding the third variable

Confounders are not always in the file, and the ones that are can usually be found by asking three questions.

Which column could plausibly influence both variables? In the penguin case, species influences both bill measurements. Species was an obvious candidate because it is the most fundamental grouping in the file.

Does the relationship survive when you split by that column? This is the actual test. Compute the correlation within each group and compare it against the pooled figure. Agreement means the grouping is not driving the relationship. Disagreement, in strength or in sign, means it is.

What changed at the same time? For anything measured over time, a correlation between two trending series often reflects nothing but the fact that both are trending. Sales and temperatures both rise through summer.

## What does establish causation

Only two routes are reliable, and the first is far stronger.

A randomised experiment breaks confounding by construction. Assign at random and the groups end up alike in every respect except the one you control, including variables you never measured. This is what an A/B test does, and it is why it supports a causal claim that no observational comparison can.

Where randomisation is impossible, causal claims rest on an accumulation of evidence: a consistent relationship across different populations, a plausible mechanism, a dose-response pattern where more exposure gives more effect, correct temporal order, and the elimination of the confounders anyone can name. That accumulation is how smoking was established as a cause of lung cancer, and it took decades.

## What to write when you cannot establish it

The honest report is not silence. It is a description of the association together with what it does not settle.

Say that the two variables are associated, give the strength, name the confounders you checked and ruled out, and name the ones you could not check. A sentence like "attendance and marks are associated, and this comparison cannot separate that from students who are motivated doing more of both" is more useful than either an unhedged causal claim or a refusal to say anything.

## Practise finding the third variable

Take three correlations you have heard asserted as causal: a habit and an outcome, a policy and a result, a product feature and a sale.

For each, write down one plausible common cause and one plausible reverse-causation story.

Then take a dataset with a grouping column and repeat the penguin exercise: compute a correlation across the whole file, then within each group, and compare. If the two disagree, you have found on your own data the thing that makes this topic worth a chapter.
