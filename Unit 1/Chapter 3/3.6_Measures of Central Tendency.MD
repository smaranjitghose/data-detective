## One number to stand in for many

Someone asks what a penguin in the Palmer file weighs. There are 344 answers and none of them is the answer they want.

They want one number that represents the group. Three such numbers exist, they disagree with each other, and choosing between them is a decision about what you are trying to convey rather than a matter of correctness.

Work with the first nine birds that have a recorded mass, in grams:

```
3750, 3800, 3250, 3450, 3650, 3625, 4675, 3475, 4250
```

## The mean

The mean adds every value and divides by how many there are.

$$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$$

Here $\bar{x}$ is the mean of the sample, $n$ is the number of values, $x_i$ is the $i$th value, and the summation sign says to add every value from the first to the $n$th.

For those nine masses, the total is 33,925 grams and $n$ is 9, giving a mean of 3,769.44 grams.

Every value contributes to that answer, which is the mean's strength and its weakness. The 4,675 gram bird pulls the mean upward on its own. Remove it and the mean of the remaining eight drops by more than a hundred grams, without any of the other birds changing.

## The median

The median is the middle value once the numbers are in order. Sort the nine masses:

```
3250, 3450, 3475, 3625, 3650, 3750, 3800, 4250, 4675
```

With nine values the middle position is the fifth, so the median is 3,650 grams. With an even count there is no single middle, and the median is the average of the two central values.

The median already sits 119 grams below the mean, and the reason is the pair of heavy birds at the top. The median does not care how heavy they are. Replace 4,675 with 8,000 and the median stays at 3,650, while the mean climbs past 4,100.

> The mean asks how the total is shared out. The median asks what the typical member looks like. When those two answers differ, the gap is telling you the distribution is lopsided.

## The mode

The mode is the value that occurs most often. Among those nine masses no value repeats, so there is no useful mode, which is normal for continuous measurements.

The mode earns its place with categorical columns, where the other two do not apply at all. The most common species in the file is Adelie, with 152 birds against 124 Gentoo and 68 Chinstrap. No mean or median of species exists. The mode is the only summary available.

## Choosing between them

| Situation | Use | Why |
| --------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Roughly symmetric values, no extremes | Mean | Uses all the information |
| A few very large or very small values | Median | Unaffected by how extreme they are |
| Income, house prices, city populations | Median | These are almost always stretched at the top |
| Categories with no order | Mode | The others are undefined |
| You need a total to divide up | Mean | Only the mean multiplies back to the total |

That last row is the case for the mean that people forget. Mean mass times number of birds returns the total mass. The median has no such property, which is why per-person budgets and capacity planning use means even when the values are skewed.

## Where a single number misleads

Three failures are worth recognising on sight.

Income is the standard example, and the reason median income appears in every serious report. A handful of very high earners drag the mean above what almost everyone earns, so the mean describes a person who may not exist.

Mixed groups are the second. The mean body mass across all three penguin species lands somewhere between the species, describing no species accurately. Gentoos are substantially heavier than the other two, and a single average hides that completely. Whenever a file has an obvious grouping column, compute the summary within groups before computing it overall.

Missing values are the third and the quietest. The Palmer file has rows with no mass recorded. Software will usually skip them and report a mean of whatever remains, without saying so. If the missing birds differ from the recorded ones, that mean is describing a group you did not choose.

## A check you can run in your head

Compute the mean and the median. If they are close, either summary will do. If they differ noticeably, ask which direction the gap runs.

Mean above median means a tail of large values. Mean below median means a tail of small ones. In both cases the median describes the typical case better, and the difference between the two numbers is itself worth reporting.

Try it on something you have: your monthly expenses over a year, or the marks in a subject across your class. Compute both, then find the one or two values driving the gap. Those values are usually the interesting part of the data, and a single summary is precisely what would have hidden them.
