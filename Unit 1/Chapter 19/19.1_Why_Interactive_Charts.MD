## When a static picture runs out

A scatter plot of 342 penguins shows two clusters and one point sitting away from both. On paper that is where the chart's usefulness ends: you can see the point and you cannot ask which bird it is.

An interactive chart answers that by hovering. The point reports its species, island, sex, and measurements without any change to the chart.

That is the whole case for interactivity. It lets a reader ask follow-up questions the chart's author did not anticipate.

## What interaction actually adds

| Interaction | Answers |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Hover | Which record is this, and what else is true of it |
| Zoom and pan | What is happening in this crowded region |
| Legend toggling | What does the chart look like without this group |
| Selection | What are the values of the points I have picked |
| Animation | How did this change over time |

Legend toggling is the most underrated. Clicking a species in the legend removes it from the chart, so a reader can isolate two groups from five without anyone rebuilding anything.

Zoom matters whenever the data spans very different densities. A time series of two years at daily resolution is a smear at full width and legible when zoomed to a month, and a static chart has to choose one of those in advance.

## Where interactivity is the wrong answer

It is not free, and three costs are worth stating.

An interactive chart cannot be printed, pasted into a slide, or included in a PDF without becoming a static image, at which point the interaction is gone and the chart may be worse than one designed to be static.

It requires the reader to do something. A chart whose finding is only visible after hovering over the right point has hidden its finding, and most readers will not look.

And it invites exploration that the analyst should have done. Publishing an interactive chart instead of an answer moves work onto the reader, which is occasionally generous and usually an abdication.

> Interactivity is for the reader who wants to check something. The finding still has to be visible without touching anything.

## The right occasions

Three, in practice.

Your own exploration, where hovering to identify an outlier is faster than filtering the DataFrame to find it. This is the most common use and it never leaves your notebook.

A dashboard someone consults repeatedly, where the questions vary between visits and the data updates.

And a chart where the natural resolution exceeds a screen: geographic data, long time series, thousands of points with meaningful labels.

For a finding in a report, a well-labelled static chart is usually better, because it makes the point without requiring anything of the reader.

## What it costs technically

```python
fig.write_html('chart.html')                          # 4,760 KB
fig.write_html('chart.html', include_plotlyjs='cdn')  # 22.6 KB
```

An interactive chart carries its own machinery. By default the file embeds the entire plotting library, which is nearly five megabytes for one scatter plot.

Setting `include_plotlyjs='cdn'` loads the library from the internet instead, cutting the file to 23 KB, at the cost of the chart being blank without a connection.

That trade-off summarises the format: interactive charts live on the web, and they behave like web pages rather than like documents.

## Where Plotly sits

Matplotlib and seaborn produce static images. Plotly produces HTML and JavaScript, which is why the interaction works in a browser and why the output is not an image file.

The libraries do not replace each other. Most working analysts use seaborn for exploration and reports, and Plotly when interaction earns its place.

## Practise the judgement

Take three charts you have made.

For each, write down one question a reader might have that the chart cannot answer, and decide whether hovering, zooming, or filtering would answer it.

Then ask whether that question is important enough to justify a chart that cannot be printed. Usually one of the three qualifies, and identifying which is the entire decision.
