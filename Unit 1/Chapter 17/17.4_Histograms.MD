## The shape of one column

A histogram divides a numeric range into intervals and counts how many values fall in each. It is the standard way to see a distribution, and everything the statistics units said about shape becomes visible in one picture.

```python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv('penguins.csv')

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 4))
ax.hist(df['body_mass_g'].dropna(), bins=10, edgecolor='white')
ax.set_xlabel('Body mass (g)')
ax.set_ylabel('Number of penguins')
```

With 10 bins across the range 2,700 to 6,300 grams, the counts are:

```
15, 43, 71, 53, 42, 41, 28, 27, 16, 6
```

The bins are 360 grams wide. The tallest sits around 3,400 to 3,800 grams, the counts fall away, and there is a second slower rise around 4,900 to 5,200 before the tail.

That second rise is the Gentoos. The bimodality the course established statistically is visible here as a shoulder in the shape.

## The bin count changes what you see

The same 342 values, binned three ways:

| Bins | Counts |
| ---: | ------------------------------------- |
| 5 | 58, 124, 83, 55, 22 |
| 10 | 15, 43, 71, 53, 42, 41, 28, 27, 16, 6 |
| 30 | 1, 7, 7, 8, 11, 24, 22, 24, 25, 15, 26, 12, 16, 12, 14, 11, 17, 13, 10, 9, 9, 7, 9, 11, 5, 8, 3, 5, 0, 1 |

At 5 bins the shape is a single smooth hump and the second group has disappeared entirely into one wide bin.

At 30 bins the counts jump around: 26 then 12 then 16 then 12. Most of that movement is noise, since a bin holding a dozen birds moves by several whenever one bird shifts.

At 10 bins the structure is visible without the noise. Nothing about the data changed; only the number of bins.

> A histogram is not a property of the data. It is a property of the data and your bin choice, and the second one is yours.

The practical response is to try several. Draw the same column at 10, 20, and 40 bins and see which features persist. A feature that appears at one bin count and vanishes at the next is not a feature.

## Bin edges matter too

Beyond the count, where the boundaries fall changes the picture. Two histograms with the same number of bins, offset slightly, can put a peak in different places.

```python
ax.hist(values, bins=[2500, 3000, 3500, 4000, 4500, 5000, 5500, 6000, 6500])
```

Passing explicit edges is worth doing when the boundaries mean something, such as round numbers a reader can hold in mind, or thresholds that matter in the domain.

## Comparing groups

```python
for species in ['Adelie', 'Chinstrap', 'Gentoo']:
    subset = df[df['species'] == species]['body_mass_g'].dropna()
    ax.hist(subset, bins=15, alpha=0.5, label=species)
ax.legend()
```

Three overlaid histograms with transparency. The Adelie and Chinstrap distributions sit on top of each other between roughly 3,000 and 4,500 grams, and the Gentoo distribution sits to the right with limited overlap.

Overlaid histograms work for two or three groups and become a mess beyond that. The alternative is one small panel per group with a shared axis, which is the subject of the subplots topic.

## Density instead of counts

```python
ax.hist(subset, bins=15, density=True)
```

With `density=True` the bars are scaled so the total area is 1, which makes groups of different sizes comparable. Comparing 68 Chinstraps against 152 Adelies by raw counts shows mainly that there are more Adelies.

The vertical axis is then a density rather than a count, and it is not a proportion. Label it accordingly.

## What to read off a histogram

Four things, all of which the statistics units named.

Where the bulk sits, which is the centre. How wide it is, which is the spread. Whether it is symmetric or has a tail on one side. And whether there is one peak or several, since a second peak means a group you have not separated.

## Practise the bin sweep

Take a numeric column and draw it at 5, 10, 20, and 50 bins.

Write down which features are present in all four and which appear in only one. The persistent ones are the shape; the rest is the bin choice.

Then split the column by a category and overlay the groups. If the pooled shape had a second peak, this is where you find out which group it was.
